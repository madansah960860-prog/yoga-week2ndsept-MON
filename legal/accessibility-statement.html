Accessibility statement

First Light Yoga is committed to making this site usable by as many people as possible, including people who use screen readers, keyboard navigation, magnification, voice control or reduced motion settings.

Last updated 09/01/2025.

Conformance target

This site is built to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, WCAG 2.1, at Level AA. We consider the site to be substantially conformant, meaning it meets the standard across the pages we have tested, with the known limitations listed below.

We use WCAG 2.1 Level AA because it is the standard referenced by US federal accessibility guidance and by most state and institutional requirements, and because it is a reasonable, testable target rather than an aspiration.

What we have built in

  • Semantic structure. Every page uses real landmarks: a banner header, a primary navigation region, a main region, and a content info footer. Headings are in order and describe the content beneath them.
  • Skip to content. The first focusable item on every page is a skip link that jumps past the navigation. It is visible as soon as it receives focus.
  • Keyboard operable. Everything interactive can be reached and operated with a keyboard alone: the menu, the dropdowns, the accordions, the pose filters, the sequence player, the breath pacer, the forms and the cookie banner. The Escape key closes the menu and any open dropdown.
  • Visible focus. Focus indicators are never removed. Every focusable element shows a clear outline with an offset so it is visible against the background.
  • Contrast. Body text is set in a deep plum against a light background, comfortably above the 4.5 to 1 minimum. Larger text and interface components meet at least 3 to 1. Placeholder text and helper text are held to the body text ratio rather than being faded for style.
  • Images. Every image has descriptive alternative text stating what is actually in the photograph. Decorative background elements, including the animated gradient, are marked as hidden from assistive technology.
  • Reduced motion. If your operating system is set to reduce motion, the animated gradient stops, section reveals become instant, images do not blur in, and the breath pacer circle changes without transition. Nothing becomes unavailable.
  • Forms. Every field has a visible label, not a placeholder standing in for one. Errors appear inline next to the field they concern, in text as well as color, and are announced to assistive technology.
  • Live regions. The sequence player and the breath pacer announce pose changes and breath phases to screen readers, so neither depends on watching the screen.
  • Responsive and zoomable. The layout reflows down to 360 pixels wide and supports zoom to 200 percent without loss of content or horizontal scrolling. Pinch zoom is not disabled.
  • Text spacing. Content remains readable when text spacing, line height and letter spacing are increased by browser extensions or user stylesheets.
  • No traps. Nothing here disables the back button, blocks right-click, opens pop-ups, triggers automatic downloads or redirects you unexpectedly.

How we test

  • Keyboard-only navigation through every page, checking tab order and focus visibility.
  • Automated checks for contrast, missing alternative text, heading order and landmark structure.
  • Manual review of alternative text, because an automated tool can tell you that alt text exists but not whether it describes the image.
  • Testing at 360, 768, 1024, 1440 and 1920 pixels wide, and at 200 percent zoom.
  • Testing with the reduced motion setting enabled.

Known limitations

We would rather list these than pretend they do not exist.

  • Third-party advertising. Where display advertising is served by a programmatic partner, we control the container and its label but not the accessibility of the advertisement itself. Ads are kept out of the tab order flow of the main content as far as we are able, and are never placed between you and a control.
  • Photograph descriptions. Alternative text describes what is in each photograph. It cannot convey the full detail of a physical shape, which is why every pose is also described in words in the entry steps and alignment cues. The written instructions, not the photographs, carry the teaching.
  • The gradient background. The animated mesh is decorative and hidden from assistive technology. It stops entirely under reduced motion. If it causes discomfort and your system setting is not an option for you, please tell us and we will look at adding an on-page control.
  • Screen reader coverage. Testing to date has focused on keyboard operation and automated checks. Broader testing with a range of screen readers is planned, and reports from people using them are the most valuable thing we receive.

Accessibility of the practice itself

Accessibility on this site is not only about the web pages. The instructional content is written on the principle that chair, wall and prop-supported versions of a pose are equal alternatives rather than lesser ones, and they are given the same detail and the same word count as the floor version.

Roughly two thirds of the pose library has a chair or standing version written out in full, and the Desk Reset sequence is a complete practice done entirely seated. The props guide sets out how to build a whole practice at chair height.

Report a problem

If any part of this site does not work for you, please tell us. We treat accessibility reports as bugs, not as feedback.

What helps us fix it faster: the page address, what you were trying to do, what happened instead, and what you were using, including browser, operating system and any assistive technology and version.

How to reach us: email hello@firstlightyoga.com with accessibility in the subject line, call +1 (720) 555-0148 Monday to Friday between 9:00 AM and 5:00 PM Mountain Time, use the contact form with the accessibility topic selected, or write to First Light Yoga LLC, 1412 Pearl Street, Suite 204, Boulder, CO 80302, United States.

What happens next. We acknowledge accessibility reports within two business days. We aim to fix anything that blocks access to content within ten business days, and to tell you honestly if something will take longer and why. If we cannot fix something quickly, we will offer an alternative way to get the content, including sending it to you in plain text or as a large-print document.

Formal complaints

If you are not satisfied with our response, tell us and we will escalate it internally and reply in writing. You may also contact the US Department of Justice Civil Rights Division, which enforces the Americans with Disabilities Act, or the relevant authority in your jurisdiction.
