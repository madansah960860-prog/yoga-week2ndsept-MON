Health and safety disclaimer

Everything published on First Light Yoga is general movement education. It is not medical advice, it is not a diagnosis, it is not treatment, and it is not a substitute for care from a qualified healthcare professional.

Last updated 09/01/2025. This page is linked from every pose page and every sequence page on the site, not only from the footer.

The essentials

  • This is education, not healthcare. Nothing here diagnoses, treats, cures, heals, relieves or prevents any condition, and nothing here claims to.
  • Speak to a professional before you start. Consult a qualified healthcare professional before beginning any new physical practice, and particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical, or managing a health condition.
  • Stop if you feel pain. Sharp, pinching, burning or radiating sensation is a signal to come out of a shape, not to breathe into it.
  • Work with a qualified teacher in person. A written guide cannot see you. Hands-on guidance from a teacher watching you is different in kind from anything a website can offer.
  • You practice at your own risk. Physical activity carries a risk of injury. By using this material you accept that risk.

Reading this site, emailing us, or subscribing to anything does not create a doctor and patient relationship, a therapist and client relationship, or a teacher and student relationship. We cannot and will not answer questions about whether a specific pose is safe for your specific condition, and we will tell you so if you ask.

Speak with a professional first if any of these apply

This is not an exhaustive list. It is a list of the situations where a conversation with a professional who knows your history matters most before you begin.

  • You are pregnant, recently gave birth, or are trying to conceive.
  • You have had surgery in the last twelve months, particularly to the spine, abdomen, hip, knee, shoulder or wrist.
  • You have a current injury, or a recurring one that has not been assessed.
  • You have a heart condition, high or low blood pressure, or have been advised to limit exertion.
  • You have a respiratory condition such as asthma or COPD.
  • You have osteoporosis, osteopenia, or have been told to avoid spinal flexion or loaded twisting.
  • You have a disc condition, spinal stenosis, or nerve symptoms in an arm or leg.
  • You have glaucoma or raised eye pressure, or have been advised to avoid head-below-heart positions.
  • You have a balance disorder, inner-ear symptoms, neuropathy, or a history of falls.
  • You have epilepsy or a history of seizures. This matters particularly for breath retention practices.
  • You have a joint replacement, or a joint that dislocates.
  • You are recovering from a recent illness, or you are taking medication that affects your balance, blood pressure or alertness.

If a professional advises against something described here, follow their advice, not ours. They can examine you and we cannot.

Contraindications on individual pages

Every pose page on this site carries a contraindication note naming who should approach that shape with care or avoid it, and every sequence page carries specific safety notes for the shapes it contains. Those notes are general information written for a general audience. They are a starting point for a conversation with a professional, not a substitute for one.

Two specific notes that recur across the site and are worth repeating here. Extended time lying flat on the back is generally discouraged in the second and third trimesters of pregnancy. Breath retention practices, meaning holding the breath in or out, are not suitable for everyone, and the breathwork page lists who should avoid them.

When to stop, immediately

Come out of whatever you are doing and stop practicing if you experience any of the following.

  • Chest pain, chest tightness, or pain radiating into the jaw or an arm.
  • Shortness of breath that is out of proportion to what you are doing.
  • Dizziness, lightheadedness, or feeling faint.
  • Sharp, sudden or radiating pain anywhere.
  • Numbness, tingling, or loss of strength in a limb.
  • Sudden severe headache, visual changes, or confusion.
  • Nausea beyond mild queasiness.
  • Any joint that gives way or feels unstable.

If symptoms are severe or do not resolve quickly, seek medical attention. In an emergency in the United States, call 911. Do not wait to see whether it passes.

What we do not claim

We think it is worth being explicit about this, because a great deal of yoga writing is not.

  • We do not claim any pose, sequence or breathing pattern treats, cures, heals, relieves or prevents any condition. That includes back pain, neck pain, anxiety, depression, insomnia, digestion, blood pressure, fertility, immunity, hormone levels or anything else.
  • We do not make weight-loss claims. There are no calorie figures, no fat-burning language, no toning or slimming claims, no before-and-after content and no body-shape commentary anywhere on this site.
  • We do not publish diet, cleanse, fasting or detox content, and we do not pair movement with dietary restriction.
  • We do not claim any therapeutic effect from a prop, a style, a breath pattern or a duration of practice.

Where we describe what a pose does, we describe it physically: which joints move, which muscles lengthen or work, where load is carried. That is what we can honestly say.

Practicing at home, alone

Nobody is watching you, so build your own margins.

  • Clear the floor and check the wall before you start.
  • Check that a mat, rug or cushion stack cannot slide before you put weight on it.
  • Do not stand on foam blocks.
  • Place a chair against a wall for anything you press into.
  • Stay within a range you could stop from at any moment.
  • Come up slowly from the floor, and pause sitting before standing.
  • Never practice breathing exercises in water, while driving, or anywhere you could fall.
