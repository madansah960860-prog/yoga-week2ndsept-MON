Pose library

Thirty-two poses with their English and Sanskrit names, the difficulty we would give them, the areas of the body most involved and the props that make each one workable. Six poses have a complete written guide, linked by name.

  • Mountain Pose

    Tāḍāsana

    Standing evenly on both feet with the spine long. The reference shape every other standing pose is measured against.

    • Beginner
    • Full guide

  • Downward-Facing Dog

    Adho Mukha Śvānāsana

    An inverted V on hands and feet. Loads the shoulders and wrists while the backs of the legs lengthen.

    • Beginner
    • Full guide

  • Warrior Two

    Vīrabhadrāsana II

    A wide stance with the front knee bent over the ankle and both arms reaching horizontally.

    • Beginner
    • Full guide

  • Tree Pose

    Vṛkṣāsana

    A single-leg balance with the lifted foot resting against the standing leg's calf or inner thigh.

    • Beginner
    • Full guide

  • Child's Pose

    Bālāsana

    A kneeling fold with the torso resting toward the thighs. Used as a rest between stronger shapes.

    • Beginner
    • Full guide

  • Bridge Pose

    Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana

    Lying on the back with the knees bent, lifting the hips so the front of the body lengthens.

    • Beginner
    • Full guide

  • Cat and Cow

    Mārjārīāsana and Bitilāsana

    On hands and knees, alternating between rounding and arching the spine in time with the breath. Also available seated in a chair.

    • Beginner
    • Blanket under knees

  • Standing Forward Fold

    Uttānāsana

    Folding forward from the hips with the knees soft, letting the head hang. Hands land on blocks, shins or the seat of a chair.

    • Beginner
    • Blocks under hands

  • Half Forward Fold

    Ardha Uttānāsana

    A halfway lift from a forward fold with the spine long and the hands on the shins or blocks. Teaches the difference between folding and rounding.

    • Beginner
    • Blocks or chair back

  • Low Lunge

    Añjaneyāsana

    One foot forward, the back knee down, hips settling toward the floor. The front of the back thigh lengthens.

    • Beginner
    • Blanket under back knee

  • High Lunge

    Aśva Sañcalanāsana

    A long stance with the back heel lifted and the back leg straight. More demanding on the front thigh than the low version.

    • Intermediate
    • Blocks under hands

  • Warrior One

    Vīrabhadrāsana I

    A lunge with the back foot turned in, hips facing forward and arms overhead. Asks a lot of the back ankle.

    • Intermediate
    • Shorten the stance

  • Triangle Pose

    Trikoṇāsana

    A wide stance with both legs straight, the torso tipping sideways over the front leg and the lower hand reaching down.

    • Intermediate
    • Hand on a block

  • Extended Side Angle

    Utthita Pārśvakoṇāsana

    From a wide stance with the front knee bent, the torso leans over the front thigh and the top arm reaches past the ear.

    • Intermediate
    • Forearm on thigh

  • Chair Pose

    Utkaṭāsana

    Feet together or hip width, knees bending as if sitting back onto a stool, arms lifting. Works the fronts of the thighs.

    • Beginner
    • Back against a wall

  • Wide-Legged Forward Fold

    Prasārita Pādottānāsana

    Feet wide and parallel, folding forward from the hips with the hands on the floor or blocks between the feet.

    • Beginner
    • Blocks or chair seat

  • Warrior Three

    Vīrabhadrāsana III

    Balancing on one leg with the torso and back leg reaching toward horizontal. Demanding on the standing hip.

    • Intermediate
    • Hands on a wall

  • Half Moon

    Ardha Candrāsana

    A standing balance on one leg with the other leg lifted parallel to the floor and the torso turned open.

    • Intermediate
    • Block and wall

  • Eagle Pose

    Garuḍāsana

    Arms and legs wrapped across the midline while balancing on one foot. The upper back spreads wide.

    • Intermediate
    • Seated in a chair

  • Plank Pose

    Phalakāsana

    A straight line from head to heels supported on the hands and toes. The whole front of the body works to hold the line.

    • Intermediate
    • Knees down or hands on a wall

  • Cobra Pose

    Bhujaṅgāsana

    Lying face down, lifting the chest with the hands lightly loaded. A small range done well beats a big range forced.

    • Beginner
    • Blanket under the hips

  • Sphinx Pose

    Salamba Bhujaṅgāsana

    Propped on the forearms face down, chest lifted, held for longer than Cobra because the arms carry the weight.

    • Beginner
    • Bolster under the chest

  • Locust Pose

    Śalabhāsana

    Face down, lifting the chest, arms and legs away from the floor without pushing with the hands. Back and glute muscles work.

    • Intermediate
    • Lift one limb at a time

  • Seated Forward Fold

    Paścimottānāsana

    Sitting with the legs straight ahead, folding forward from the hip creases. A strap around the feet removes the need to reach.

    • Beginner
    • Strap around the feet

  • Head-to-Knee Pose

    Jānu Śīrṣāsana

    One leg straight, the other foot drawn to the inner thigh, folding over the straight leg. Each side feels different, and that is normal.

    • Beginner
    • Sit on a folded blanket

  • Bound Angle Pose

    Baddha Koṇāsana

    Seated with the soles of the feet together and the knees dropping wide. Blocks under the knees make it restful rather than effortful.

    • Beginner
    • Blocks under the knees

  • Seated Twist

    Ardha Matsyendrāsana

    Sitting tall and rotating the ribcage to one side, with one hand behind and the other outside the opposite thigh.

    • Beginner
    • Chair version

  • Supine Twist

    Supta Matsyendrāsana

    Lying on the back and letting both bent knees drop to one side with the arms wide. Held long and quietly.

    • Beginner
    • Bolster under the knees

  • Reclining Hand-to-Big-Toe

    Supta Pādāṅguṣṭhāsana

    On the back with one leg lifted, a strap around the foot. The most controllable way to work with the back of the leg.

    • Beginner
    • Strap around the foot

  • Legs Up the Wall

    Viparīta Karaṇī

    Lying on the back with the legs resting vertically against a wall. Almost no effort required once you are in it.

    • Beginner
    • Blanket under the hips

  • Pigeon Preparation

    Eka Pāda Rājakapotāsana, preparation

    One shin forward and angled, the other leg long behind. Strong through the outer hip, and easy to overdo without support.

    • Intermediate
    • Block under the hip

  • Corpse Pose

    Śavāsana

    Lying flat and still at the end of a practice, arms slightly away from the body. Doing nothing on purpose is a skill.

    • Beginner
    • Bolster under the knees

How difficulty is judged here

Difficulty on this site describes coordination and load, not flexibility. A pose is beginner when it has few moving parts, a clear way in and out, and a supported version that keeps the same intent. It is intermediate when it asks you to balance, to carry body weight on the arms, or to coordinate two things at once. It is advanced when it needs a base of the earlier shapes before it makes sense at all.

None of that is a ladder you are supposed to climb. Plenty of experienced practitioners spend most of their time in shapes labeled beginner, which is exactly what those shapes are for.
