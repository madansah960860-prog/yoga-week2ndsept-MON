Sequence

Desk Reset

  • Total 8 minutes
  • Beginner
  • One sturdy chair
  • 12 holds

Twelve holds done entirely in a chair. No mat, no floor, no change of clothes. It fits between two meetings and it is quiet enough to do in a shared office without anybody looking twice, apart from possibly the last one.

This is also the sequence to use if getting down to and up from the floor is difficult, for any reason at all. It is a complete practice in its own right, not a reduced version of a floor practice.

The chair matters

Use a chair that does not roll and does not swivel. A dining chair or a stacking office chair is right. A wheeled desk chair is not, because half of these shapes involve pressing into the seat or leaning your weight sideways, and a chair that moves under you turns a stretch into a balance problem.

Sit toward the front third of the seat for most of the sequence, with both feet flat on the floor and the knees roughly over the ankles. If your feet do not reach the floor comfortably, put a book or two under them. If the seat is deep, a cushion behind your lower back brings you forward.

What it covers

Spinal flexion and extension, side bending in both directions, rotation in both directions, shoulder work across the front of the body, outer hip on both sides, and a chair version of a standing pose. That is most of the directions a spine and a pair of hips can move in, in eight minutes.

Beginner modification path

  • Seated Cat and Cow: reduce the range by half. The movement should feel easy, not like an effort.
  • Side Bend: keep the lifted hand on the shoulder rather than reaching overhead if the shoulder is tight.
  • Seated Twist: hold the outside of the opposite thigh rather than the chair back, and turn only as far as the ribs go without the hips following.
  • Eagle Arms: if the forearms do not wrap, cross the arms and hold opposite shoulders instead. Same spread across the upper back.
  • Figure Four: if the ankle does not reach the opposite knee, rest it on the shin instead, or place the foot on a low stool with the knee turned out slightly.
  • Chair Warrior Two: keep the extended leg's knee slightly bent and the foot on the floor.
  • Seated Forward Fold: rest the forearms on the thighs rather than folding all the way down.

Doing this more than once a day

There is nothing in this sequence that becomes a problem if you repeat it. If you sit for most of a working day, running poses two through six once mid-morning and the whole thing once mid-afternoon is a reasonable pattern.

What matters more than frequency is that you actually stop. The reason a movement break works is that it interrupts a static position, and doing it at your desk while reading email interrupts nothing. Turn away from the screen.

A three-minute version

Poses one, two, five, six and twelve. Spine forward and back, rotation both ways, and a fold to finish. Three minutes, still worth doing.

Before you practice this sequence

This sequence is general movement education. It is not medical advice and it makes no claim to treat, prevent or improve any condition, including any condition associated with sitting. Speak with a qualified healthcare professional before beginning a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, recovering from surgery or managing a health condition.

Use a chair that cannot roll or swivel. Stop if you feel pain, particularly in the twists, and never force rotation. For hands-on guidance, work with a qualified teacher in person.
