Pose guide

Bridge Pose

Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana, pronounced SAY-too BAHN-duh sar-vahn-GAHS-uh-nuh

  • Beginner
  • On the back
  • Block option
  • Hold 20 to 45 seconds

Lying on your back with the knees bent, lifting the hips so the front of the body lengthens and the back of the legs work. It is the most accessible back-bending shape in a beginner practice because the floor is right there underneath you, and coming out of it takes half a second.

Entry steps

  1. Lie on your back. Knees bent, feet flat on the floor, arms alongside the body with the palms down.
  2. Set the feet. Walk the heels toward the hips until your fingertips can just brush them. Feet hip-width apart and parallel, second toes pointing straight ahead.
  3. Press the arms down. Press the backs of the upper arms and the palms into the floor. This is where the shape gets its stability.
  4. Lift on an exhale. Press through the feet and lift the hips, peeling the spine off the floor from the tailbone upward.
  5. Stop at the shoulder blades. The weight ends up on the feet, the arms and the upper back. Never on the neck.
  6. Roll the shoulders under. Shift each shoulder a little further beneath you, then either clasp the hands under the back or leave the palms flat.
  7. Keep the knees in line. Knees stay hip-width and pointing forward. They will want to splay outward.
  8. Come down on an exhale. Lower from the upper back downward, one vertebra at a time, hips last. Rest for two breaths before repeating.

Alignment cues

  • Shins vertical at the top. If the knees end up ahead of the ankles, walk the feet closer.
  • Knees hip-width. Squeeze a block between the thighs if they drift apart.
  • Neck neutral and still. Set the head before you lift and do not turn it while you are up there.
  • Chest toward the chin, not chin toward the chest. The lift comes from the upper back, not from tucking the head.
  • Even feet. Press the inner and outer edges of both feet down equally, particularly the big toe mound.

Breath pattern

  • Lift on the exhale, which is the more stable half of the breath for this movement.
  • Breathe evenly through the nose while you hold. The front of the ribs is lengthened, so breaths may feel shallower than usual. That is expected.
  • Lower on an exhale.
  • For a dynamic version, lift on the exhale and lower on the inhale, moving continuously for six to eight rounds.

Suggested hold

  • First time: three rounds of 15 seconds with a rest between each.
  • Regular practice: two or three rounds of 30 to 45 seconds.
  • Supported on a block: 2 to 5 minutes, since nothing is working to hold you up.

Common mistakes

  • Pushing higher than the shape allows. Height is not the goal. A moderate lift with the knees in line beats a high one with the knees splayed.
  • Turning the head while lifted. The one thing to genuinely avoid. Set the head before you go up and keep it still.
  • Feet too far away. Puts all the work into the hamstrings and often produces cramping. Walk the heels in.
  • Knees splaying outward. Very common. A block between the thighs solves it immediately.
  • Clenching the glutes hard. Some engagement is fine, gripping tightly is not. Press the feet down and let the lift follow.
  • Dropping down all at once. Lower slowly. The controlled descent is half the value of the pose.

What is actually happening

  • The muscles at the back of the thighs and the glutes work to hold the hips lifted against gravity.
  • The front of the hips and the front of the thighs lengthen as the pelvis lifts.
  • The upper back extends slightly while the shoulders move into extension underneath you.
  • The whole front of the torso lengthens, which is why the breath feels different in this shape than it does lying flat.

Modifications

The supported version below is a genuinely different experience from the active one, and both are worth having in a practice.

Block under the sacrum

Lift the hips, slide a block under the flat bony plate at the base of the spine, then rest your weight down onto it. Use the lowest height first. Nothing has to work to keep you up, so you can stay for several minutes. Take the block out before lowering, rather than sliding off it.

Block between the thighs

Hold a block lengthwise between the upper inner thighs and squeeze gently throughout. It keeps the knees hip-width without you having to think about it, and gives the inner thighs a clear job.

Strap around the thighs

Loop a strap around both thighs just above the knees, tightened to hip width, and press outward into it. This works the opposite way to the block and suits anyone whose knees collapse inward rather than outward.

Blanket under the shoulders

Fold a firm blanket and place it under the shoulders so the head and neck rest slightly lower than the shoulders. This gives the neck more room and is a standard setup for anyone with neck sensitivity.

Small lift only

Lift the hips only an inch or two off the floor and hold there. The hamstrings and glutes still work, the back barely extends, and the shape stays entirely undemanding.

Seated alternative in a chair

If getting to the floor is not practical, sit tall on a chair with the feet flat, place the hands on the seat behind the hips, and gently lift the chest while drawing the shoulder blades toward each other. You get the front-of-chest lengthening and the upper-back extension without going to the floor.

Progression

  • Hands clasped underneath. Interlace the fingers under your back and walk the shoulders further under you. More work for the upper back and shoulders.
  • Dynamic rounds. Lift on the exhale, lower on the inhale, eight times continuously. Builds hamstring and glute endurance.
  • One leg extended. From a stable lift, straighten one leg toward the ceiling while keeping both hips level. Significantly harder than it looks.
  • Heels lifted. Rise onto the balls of both feet while holding the lift, which shifts more work into the hamstrings.

This pose appears in the 10-Minute Morning sequence, and in a supported form in the Evening Wind-Down.

Contraindications and care

Approach with care or use the small-lift or chair version if: you have neck pain or a history of neck injury, shoulder restriction that makes lying on the back of the shoulders uncomfortable, a recent lower-back injury, or knee sensitivity.

Skip it and speak with a professional first if: you have a cervical spine condition, a disc issue that has been aggravated by back extension, are recovering from surgery to the spine, shoulder, hip or abdomen, or you are in the second or third trimester of pregnancy, when extended time lying flat on the back is generally discouraged.

Do not turn the head while the hips are lifted. If you feel any pinching in the lower back, lower the hips until it stops. Work with a qualified teacher in person for hands-on guidance.
