Sequence

10-Minute Morning

  • Total 10 minutes
  • Beginner
  • No props required
  • 14 holds

Fourteen holds that start folded on the floor, pass through four standing shapes, and finish flat on your back. It is designed for the first ten minutes of a day, in whatever you slept in, on carpet if you have it and on a mat if you do not.

Read the whole order once before your first attempt so you are not stopping to check what comes next. After three or four runs you will know it, and that is when the sequence starts being useful rather than just being followed.

What you need

  • Floor space roughly 6 feet by 3 feet, enough to lie down with the arms slightly out.
  • A mat if your floor is hard or slippery. On carpet you can skip it.
  • Optional: a folded blanket if kneeling on a hard floor is uncomfortable, and a pair of blocks if your hands do not reach the floor in a standing fold.

The arc of it

Three minutes of floor warm-up, four minutes standing, three minutes back down. Nothing in it requires you to balance on one leg, hold weight on your wrists for long, or fold any further than is comfortable.

Beginner modification path

If you are new, run the sequence this way for the first two weeks. It is the same practice with the demands lowered.

  • Child's Pose: knees wide, a folded blanket between the calves and thighs, forehead on stacked fists.
  • Downward-Facing Dog: hands on the seat of a chair against a wall instead of on the floor.
  • Standing Forward Fold: hands on blocks, on the shins, or on a chair seat. Knees generously bent.
  • Warrior Two: shorten the stance and bend the front knee only to forty-five degrees. Hands on the hips if holding the arms out is tiring.
  • Chair Pose: stand with your back against a wall and slide down only a few inches.
  • Bridge Pose: lift the hips one or two inches only, or rest them on a block at its lowest height.
  • Supine Twist: put a cushion between the knees and let them rest on a second cushion at the side.

Notes on the order

The first two shapes are on the floor because most people are stiffer first thing and starting upright asks for range that is not there yet. Child's Pose gives the hips and knees a slow fold, and Cat and Cow moves the spine through its two main directions before anything is loaded.

The standing block goes fold, stand, warrior, warrior, chair, fold. That order takes you from the least demanding standing shape to the most, then returns to the fold, which is a natural place to come back down to the floor from.

Bridge is placed after the standing work because the front of the hips has been in flexion and the hamstrings have been working, so the shape has something to do. The two twists undo the rotation the standing shapes introduced. Corpse Pose at the end is short here at forty-five seconds; if you have the time, take two or three minutes instead.

If you have fewer than ten minutes

Do poses one, two, three, five, six, seven and fourteen. That is a five-minute version with the same arc. Skipping the end rest is the one edit not worth making.

Before you practice this sequence

This sequence is general movement education. It is not medical advice and it makes no claim to treat, prevent or improve any condition. Speak with a qualified healthcare professional before beginning a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, recovering from surgery or managing a health condition.

Stop if you feel pain. Sharp or radiating sensation is a signal to come out of a shape, not to work through it. If you feel lightheaded when you stand up from the floor, sit for a few breaths first. For hands-on guidance, work with a qualified teacher in person.
