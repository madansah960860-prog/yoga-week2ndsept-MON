Sequence

Evening Wind-Down

  • Total 15 minutes
  • Beginner
  • Blanket, bolster or pillows, wall
  • 10 holds

Ten holds, none shorter than a minute, all of them either seated or on the back and all of them supported by something. Nothing here needs strength or balance, and the longest single hold is three minutes with your legs up a wall.

Long holds feel different from short ones. The first thirty seconds are usually the least comfortable, and the shape settles after that. Resist the urge to keep adjusting; set the props carefully at the start and then leave yourself alone.

Setting up before you start

Gather everything first. Stopping in the middle to find a second cushion undoes most of the point of the sequence.

  • A bolster, or two firm bed pillows stacked and pressed into a long shape. A rolled duvet works.
  • A firm blanket, folded into a rectangle about four inches thick, to sit on.
  • A wall with four feet of clear floor along it. Move the furniture before you begin.
  • Optional: a strap or a bathrobe belt, one block, and a second blanket to put over you. Bodies cool down quickly when they stop moving.

Beginner modification path

  • Supported Child's Pose: if kneeling is not comfortable, sit on a chair and fold forward with the forehead on a stack of cushions on a table.
  • Seated folds: sit up on a higher stack of blankets. The higher the hips, the easier the fold. Bend the knees as much as you like.
  • Head-to-Knee Pose: put a rolled blanket under the bent knee if it hovers off the floor.
  • Bound Angle: blocks or firm cushions under both knees, and sit against a wall if the spine wants a backrest.
  • Supine Twist: a cushion between the knees and a second one under the knees where they land.
  • Legs Up the Wall: if getting the legs up is awkward, put the lower legs on the seat of a chair instead. Same shape, much easier entry.
  • Supported Bridge: skip the block entirely and simply lie with the knees bent and feet flat.

Why the holds are long

In short holds, the muscles do most of the responding. In holds of a minute or more, with the body fully supported so nothing has to work, the sensation tends to change character: the initial pull settles, and what you notice shifts from the muscle toward the whole area around the joint.

That is why the props matter so much here. A supported shape you can stay in for two minutes is a different practice from an unsupported version of the same shape you can hold for twenty seconds. Neither is better, but this sequence is built entirely around the first kind.

Making it longer

To stretch this into a half hour, double the time on poses one, five, eight and ten, and add two minutes of paced breathing from the breathwork page while you are in Legs Up the Wall. Keep the order the same.

Practicing before bed

If you practice this late, be aware that lying still in a warm room with your eyes closed is likely to make you drowsy. Set the timer on your phone as well as using the player, put the props away before you get comfortable in the last shape, and be careful standing up afterward.

Before you practice this sequence

This sequence is general movement education. It is not medical advice, it makes no claim to treat, prevent or improve any condition, and it is not a treatment for sleep difficulties or anything else. Speak with a qualified healthcare professional before beginning a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, recovering from surgery or managing a health condition.

Specific notes for this sequence. Legs Up the Wall places the legs above the heart; if you have been advised to avoid inverted positions, or if you have uncontrolled high blood pressure or glaucoma, put your lower legs on a chair seat instead. Extended time lying flat on the back is generally discouraged in the second and third trimesters of pregnancy; practice these shapes propped up on an incline or lying on your left side instead.

Stop if you feel pain, and come out of long holds slowly. For hands-on guidance, work with a qualified teacher in person.
