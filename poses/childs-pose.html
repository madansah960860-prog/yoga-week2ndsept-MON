Pose guide

Child's Pose

Bālāsana, pronounced bah-LAH-suh-nuh

  • Beginner
  • Kneeling and resting
  • Bolster or blanket helps
  • Hold 30 seconds to 3 minutes

A folded kneeling rest, used at the beginning of a practice to arrive, between stronger shapes to recover, and at the end to settle. It is the pose you are always allowed to take. In a class, dropping into it mid-sequence is a normal thing to do and no teacher worth practicing with will think anything of it.

Entry steps

  1. Kneel down. Shins on the floor, tops of the feet down, big toes touching or close behind you.
  2. Choose your knee width. Knees wide, roughly mat width, gives the belly and chest room to come between the thighs. Knees together makes a narrower, more compact fold. Both are correct versions.
  3. Sit back on an exhale. Move the hips toward the heels. If they do not reach, that is the norm, not a problem to solve today.
  4. Walk the hands forward. Let the torso lower toward the floor as the arms extend ahead of you.
  5. Rest the head. Forehead to the floor, or to stacked fists, a block or a folded blanket. The head should have something to rest on so the neck can let go.
  6. Settle the arms. Either reaching forward with the palms down, or drawn back alongside the body with the palms up. The second version lets the shoulders release more completely.
  7. Breathe into the back. Direct the breath into the back of the ribs, where you can feel it expanding against the space behind you.

Alignment cues

  • Head supported. If the forehead does not reach the floor comfortably, prop it. A head hanging in mid-air is not resting.
  • Hips heavy. Let the hips move toward the heels rather than hovering. Pad the gap if there is one.
  • Neck long. Forehead down, not chin. The back of the neck stays long.
  • Shoulders released. If the arms are forward, let the shoulders soften away from the ears rather than actively reaching.
  • Ankles comfortable. Tops of the feet flat, with a rolled towel under the ankles if that position pinches.

Breath pattern

  • Fold forward on an exhale.
  • Breathe slowly through the nose, directing the inhale into the back and sides of the ribs.
  • Let the exhale be unhurried and complete without pushing the last of the air out.
  • For a longer rest, count a four-second inhale and a six-second exhale for the first ten breaths, then let the count go.

Suggested hold

  • As a mid-practice reset: 30 seconds, or five slow breaths.
  • As a resting shape: 1 to 3 minutes.
  • Fully supported on a bolster: up to 5 minutes, turning the head halfway through.

Common mistakes

  • Hovering the forehead. Holding the head up turns a rest into neck work. Prop it.
  • Hips floating far from the heels with no support. Puts the whole weight into the knees. Put a folded blanket between the calves and the thighs.
  • Pushing the chest toward the floor. This is not a shape to deepen. Let it be where it is.
  • Painful ankles ignored. Very common and easily fixed with a rolled towel under the ankle joints.
  • Knees together when the belly is compressed. Take the knees wide. There is no virtue in the narrow version.
  • Coming out too fast. Rise slowly, rolling up through the spine with the head last.

What is actually happening

  • The hips, knees and ankles all move into deep flexion at the same time, which is what makes this shape unavailable to some bodies without props.
  • The long muscles either side of the spine lengthen as the back rounds gently.
  • With the front of the body closed and the floor in front of it, breathing naturally moves toward the back and sides of the ribcage, which is a useful thing to feel.
  • Nothing has to work to stay in the shape once you are supported, which is the entire point of it.

Modifications

This pose is only a rest if it is comfortable. Props here are not optional extras, they are how most people get the shape to do its job.

Bolster along the torso

Place a bolster, or two firm pillows in a line, lengthwise between the thighs with one end near the hips. Lie the whole front of the body along it and turn the head to one side. The torso is fully supported, nothing has to hold, and you can stay for several minutes. Turn the head the other way halfway through.

Blanket behind the knees

Roll a firm blanket and place it in the crease behind the knees before sitting back. This reduces how far the knees have to bend and takes pressure off the front of the joint. The single most useful adjustment for anyone with knee sensitivity.

Blanket under the ankles

If pointing the feet flat is uncomfortable, roll a towel and place it under the front of the ankles so the feet are not fully flattened. Small change, large difference.

Block under the forehead

Set a block at its lowest or medium height under the forehead so the head has a firm surface to rest on without straining forward. Stacked fists work the same way if there is no block nearby.

Seated fold in a chair

Sit on a chair with the feet flat and the knees wide, then fold forward and rest the forearms on the thighs or the forehead on a stack of cushions placed on a second chair or table in front of you. Same forward fold, same breathing into the back, no kneeling at all. Use this if kneeling is not available to you for any reason.

Standing forward rest

Stand a step from a table or countertop, hinge at the hips and rest the forearms and forehead on the surface with the spine long. It is a different shape mechanically but serves the same purpose in a sequence: a place to stop and breathe.

Progression

This shape does not really progress in the sense of getting harder. What changes is how completely you can let go in it, and how long you can stay. Three directions are worth exploring.

  • Longer, fully supported holds. Build from one minute toward five with a bolster.
  • Side-reaching variation. With the knees wide, walk both hands over to the right so the left side of the ribcage lengthens. Hold, then change sides.
  • Thread the needle. From hands and knees, slide one arm under the body and rest the shoulder and cheek down, which turns the resting shape into a gentle twist.

This pose closes the Evening Wind-Down sequence and appears twice in the Gentle Full Practice.

Contraindications and care

Approach with care or use the chair version if: you have knee pain, a knee replacement, ankle restriction, a recent lower-limb injury, or difficulty getting down to and up from the floor.

Modify the knee width if: you are pregnant, in which case take the knees wide so there is no pressure on the belly, and consider resting the torso on a bolster.

Skip and speak with a professional first if: you are recovering from abdominal or knee surgery, or a deeply folded position has been advised against for you.

Coming up slowly matters. If you feel lightheaded on rising, sit upright for a few breaths before standing. Work with a qualified teacher in person for hands-on guidance.
