Sequence

Gentle Full Practice

  • Total 30 minutes
  • Beginner to intermediate
  • Mat, two blocks, strap, blanket
  • 28 holds

A complete practice with a proper shape to it: five minutes of floor warm-up, eleven minutes of standing work with one balance, five minutes of floor back bending, and eight minutes of resting shapes at the end.

This is the sequence to grow into rather than start with. If you are in your first month, do the 10-Minute Morning until it is familiar, then come here. Nothing in this practice is difficult on its own; the length is what makes it a step up.

What you need

  • A mat, or a non-slip surface. Several of these shapes put weight through the hands.
  • Two blocks. Used under the hands in the standing folds and lunges, and under the hips in the last section.
  • A strap, or a bathrobe belt, for the reclining leg stretch near the end.
  • A firm blanket, to fold under the knees in kneeling shapes and to sit on later.

The four sections

  • Minutes 0 to 8: floor warm-up and lunges.
  • Minutes 8 to 17: standing shapes, ending with the balance.
  • Minutes 17 to 22: back down, floor back bending.
  • Minutes 22 to 30: reclining shapes and rest.

Beginner modification path

Run the whole sequence this way for the first few weeks. It stays thirty minutes and keeps the same arc.

  • Downward-Facing Dog: hands on the seat of a chair placed against a wall, all three times it appears.
  • Low Lunge: back knee down on a folded blanket, both hands on blocks either side of the front foot.
  • Chair Pose: back against a wall, sliding down only a few inches.
  • Warrior Two and Extended Side Angle: shorten the stance, bend the front knee to forty-five degrees, and rest the front forearm on the front thigh in the side angle.
  • Tree Pose: stand beside a wall with one hand on it, and keep the toes of the lifted foot on the floor.
  • Sphinx and Cobra: a folded blanket under the hip bones, and lift only a few inches.
  • Bridge Pose: rest the hips on a block at its lowest height instead of holding the lift.
  • Reclining leg stretch: strap around the foot, both knees as bent as you like.

Why the order is what it is

The floor warm-up comes first because the hips and spine move more freely once they have moved a little, and the lunges open the front of the hips before the standing section asks them to work in a long stance.

Standing shapes sit in the middle because that is where you have the most available energy. The balance goes at the end of that block rather than the start, on the theory that balancing when you are slightly tired is a more useful skill than balancing when you are fresh.

The floor back bending follows a rest, and comes after all the forward folding rather than mixed among it. Sphinx before Cobra because Sphinx supports more of your weight on the forearms and asks less of the arms.

The last eight minutes are all reclining and none of them require effort. Four minutes of Śavāsana is a real amount of time and it will feel long the first few times you do it. That is normal, and it is the part of a practice people most often cut and most often miss.

A 20-minute version

Skip poses 5, 6, 13, 15, 16, 20, 24 and 27, and shorten the final rest to two minutes. You lose the second side of two shapes, which is not ideal, so a better cut is to run the whole thing with every hold reduced by a third.

Before you practice this sequence

This sequence is general movement education. It is not medical advice and it makes no claim to treat, prevent or improve any condition. Speak with a qualified healthcare professional before beginning a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, recovering from surgery or managing a health condition.

Specific notes. Downward-Facing Dog appears three times and puts weight through the wrists; use the chair version throughout if your wrists object. Bridge Pose asks you to keep the head still while the hips are lifted. Extended time lying flat on the back is generally discouraged in the second and third trimesters of pregnancy.

Stop if you feel pain. Come up from the final rest slowly, rolling onto one side first and pausing there before sitting. For hands-on guidance, work with a qualified teacher in person.
